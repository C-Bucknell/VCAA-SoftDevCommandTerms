How to use this guide

This is not a generic glossary. Every command term below was found in an actual VCE Applied Computing: Software Development examination paper between 2020 and 2025, and every claim about marking comes from the VCAA assessment report for that year.

Three kinds of statement appear throughout, and they are tagged so you always know what you are reading:

PaperTaken from the wording of the examination question itself.
ReportTaken from the VCAA assessment report — what marks were actually awarded for, and what students got wrong.
TeachingOur reading of the pattern. Useful, but not something VCAA has stated. Where a teaching note and report evidence disagree, the report wins.

A word on how the paper is built. Section A is 20 multiple-choice questions. Section B is short-answer, five or six questions worth 20 marks in total. Section C is a case study, 13–19 questions worth 60 marks, and every answer in it must be applied to the case study. Command terms behave the same way in Sections B and C, but Section C adds a second requirement on top of the command term: the case study reference. That single difference accounts for a large share of the marks lost each year.

Most questions carry a compound instruction — identify and explain, state… and provide one reason, describe… and recommend. Each verb is a separate job, and the marks split across them. Missing the second verb is the most common way a strong answer ends up with half the marks.

How the marks work. VCAA awards whole marks only. There are no half marks and no partial credit within a mark: for each mark, the assessor decides that the response has met the requirement or it has not. A three-mark question is three separate decisions. This is why the phrasing throughout this guide is always one mark for… — each mark has its own requirement, and an answer that half-meets it earns nothing for that mark. It is also why two reworded versions of the same point cannot score twice: the second decision has nothing new to assess.

The six core command terms

Together these account for roughly three-quarters of the written-response marks on every paper from 2020 to 2025.

Identify

Usually 1–2 marks ~35 items · 2020–2025

Name or recognise the correct thing from your knowledge or from the material provided.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for the correct item. The mark is awarded or it is not — a nearly-right term earns nothing.
  • Report2 marks when two distinct items are required. Two versions of the same item earn one mark only.
  • Paper3–4 marks only when Identify is compounded with Explain or Describe; the extra marks belong to the second verb.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Naming something close but not the study-design term — 2024 Section B Q3a rejected "project plan", "log books", "milestones" and "critical path" where a Gantt chart was required.
  • Inventing characteristics. In 2024 Section C Q11 some students named "consistency" as a characteristic of data integrity; only the study-design list was accepted.
  • Writing the line number when asked to identify a line of pseudocode. The 2024 report states explicitly that line numbers earn no mark — you must write the line of code.
  • Adding unrequested explanation instead of a second distinct item.
What full-mark answers did
  • Used the exact term from the study design or from the stimulus material.
  • Kept the answer to a phrase — 2023 Section C Q5 wanted three data-flow labels and nothing more.
  • Where two items were asked for, gave two genuinely different things (2021 Section C Q3a: development of the database, and integration of the video conferencing system).
  • Answered the second verb separately and visibly when the question was compound.

State

Usually 1 mark per point ~18 items · 2020–2025

Give a brief, correct answer without working or elaboration.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per distinct correct point. A three-mark State question wants three separate reasons, not one reason expanded.
  • PaperWhere the paper splits the marks into labelled lines (Advantage 1, Advantage 2), each line is marked independently.
  • ReportA second point that restates the first in different words is not awarded — this is called out in the 2024 report for at least four separate questions.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Generic customer-relations statements. 2024 Section B Q2 refused marks for answers along the lines of increasing customer trust or making the customer feel good.
  • Two synonymous points on the two answer lines.
  • Giving the right answer without the reason attached. 2025 Section C Q3 found that most students named the correct data collection method, but few tied the reason to the case study.
What full-mark answers did
  • Kept each point to one sentence and made the points structurally different from one another.
  • Anchored the point in the study design: 2025 Section C Q10b accepted only the three threat-modelling principles — identifying threats, mitigating threats, confirming threats have been mitigated.
  • Answered the actual question asked. 2024 Section C Q4b required both the DFD level and a reason; the reason was the mark most students missed.

Describe

Usually 2 marks ~30 items · 2020–2025

Give an account of the features or characteristics of something, or of how something happens.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for correctly naming the feature or mechanism.
  • Report1 further mark for the detail that shows how it works, or for the link to the case study — 2024 Section C Q14b awarded its second mark purely for the case-study link.
  • Paper1-mark Describe questions do exist (2025 Section B Q1a; 2024 Section B Q6a) and want a single precise sentence.
  • Paper3-mark Describe questions carry three separate components — 2020 Section C Q13a wanted what the attack is, how it works, and its effect on the organisation.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Defining the tool instead of describing its use. 2025 Section B Q1c: many students described what a Gantt chart is rather than how it is used to update and track progress.
  • Describing generally where the question named a context. 2024 Section B Q3b noted that few students said anything specific to the scenario.
  • Two descriptions of the same feature (2024 Section C Q18a and Q18b both penalised this).
  • Claims the stimulus does not support — 2024 Section C Q18 refused cost-based comparisons because no cost information was given.
What full-mark answers did
  • Named the thing, then said what it does, then said what that means here — three moves in two or three sentences.
  • Used precise subject vocabulary: dependencies, predecessors and successors in 2025 Section B Q1a.
  • Included a worked example from the case study when the question asked for one (2025 Section C Q7a on inheritance).

Explain

Usually 2–4 marks ~33 items · 2020–2025

Make the reasons, mechanism or relationship clear — say how or why, not just what.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for the correct statement of what happens.
  • Report1 mark for the causal link — the because or which means that step.
  • ReportFurther marks for a second mechanism, or for applying the explanation to the case study.
  • Paper4-mark Explain questions usually contain two sub-items in a table (2025 Section C Q10a: two security controls, two marks each).

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Describing instead of explaining. 2022 Section B Q3 found many students listing authentication methods without explaining the purpose of authentication.
  • Stating an aim rather than a mechanism. 2025 Section C Q9 rejected answers saying user-centred design "meets user needs" without saying users are involved at every phase.
  • Leaving out the example when the question said "with examples" — 2021 Section C Q5 awarded no marks for check definitions with no example attached.
  • Explaining only one side of a comparison (2023 Section B Q3b).
What full-mark answers did
  • Wrote a chain: this happens → because of this → so the consequence here is this.
  • Used the scenario's own nouns. 2025 Section C Q10a rewarded explanations tied to the development environment, and refused ones about confidential user data that would not be there.
  • Gave the second half of a two-part comparison as much detail as the first.

Justify

Usually 3–4 marks 16 items · 2020–2025

Show, prove or defend a decision or point of view with reasoning and evidence, including why the alternative is less suitable.

The single most consistent finding in this guide. All sixteen Justify items from 2020 to 2025 placed two or more named options in front of the student. In every year, the assessment report states that a justification requires the case for the chosen option and the case against the alternative. The 2024 report says it twice, in Section C Q5 and again in Q10, using almost identical wording.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for selecting and stating a clear option.
  • Report1 mark for why that option is suitable, in terms of the scenario.
  • Report1 mark for why the alternative is not suitable — this is the mark most often lost.
  • Report1 further mark, in 4-mark items, for explicit application to the case study or to stated evaluation criteria.
  • TeachingTreat a Justify item as a four-move answer — choice, pro, con, evidence — even when it is worth 3 marks. The fourth move costs one sentence and is often what separates 2 from 3.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Arguing only for the chosen option. Reported in 2020 (C Q7, C Q8b), 2022 (C Q6b), 2024 (C Q5, C Q10) and 2025 (C Q2).
  • Ignoring the evaluation criteria supplied in the question. 2022 Section C Q6b and 2023 Section C Q9 both refused general "more user-friendly" claims.
  • Choosing the wrong option outright — 2024 Section C Q6 awarded nothing for choosing a binary search.
  • Correct facts with no case-study link. 2021 Section C Q1 noted that few students related the waterfall model back to the scenario.
What full-mark answers did
  • Named the choice on its own line before arguing.
  • Used a pivot word — whereas, however — to move into the case against the alternative.
  • Quoted the scenario's specifics: the 2025 in-house exemplar named property addresses and customer details staying inside the organisation.
  • Referred to each supplied criterion by name where criteria were given.

Complete

Usually 2–7 marks ~18 items · 2020–2025

Fill in the missing parts of a supplied diagram, table, data dictionary or algorithm so that it is correct and consistent.

The highest-value item type on the paper. Complete questions carry more marks per question than any other command term — up to 7 in 2020 and 6 in 2021, 2023 and 2025. They are also mechanical: the marks are there for anyone who knows the conventions.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per correctly filled cell, label, association or line, marked independently.
  • ReportFor test tables, 1 mark per distinct test case. Repeating a test already shown in the sample row earns nothing (2024 C Q12a, 2022 C Q12, 2025 C Q11c).
  • ReportNaming conventions are marked. 2025 Section C Q7c withheld marks where camel case was not carried through, and where "Bool" or "Float" replaced the study-design data type names.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Boundary testing not understood. 2020 Section C Q9a and 2024 Section C Q12a both required a value above, on, and below the boundary; most students tested only below it.
  • Abbreviating expected output instead of writing what the algorithm actually prints (2021 C Q7a, 2025 C Q11c).
  • Pseudocode conventions: using = instead of the assignment arrow, missing End If, no indentation (2020 B Q2b, 2024 C Q13).
  • Missing arrowheads or reversed arrows on <<includes>> and <<extends>> (2022 C Q3c).
  • Leaving the duration column or dependency arrows blank on a Gantt chart (2021 C Q2a).
What full-mark answers did
  • Tested every branch of every conditional, including nested ones.
  • Copied output strings exactly as the algorithm defines them.
  • Matched the naming convention already visible in the supplied table.
  • Used the data type and data structure names from the study design, spelled in full.

Regular command terms

Appearing in most years, and worth knowing precisely because each one has a distinct marking shape.

Suggest

Usually 1–3 marks ~14 items · 2020–2025

Put forward a workable idea or course of action for consideration.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per distinct, realistic suggestion.
  • ReportAdditional marks where the question asks the suggestion to be broken down — 2023 Section B Q5b required technique, responsibility and timing, one mark each.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Unrealistic suggestions. 2023 Section C Q13 refused marks for backing up a third-party AI service; Q7b refused "write your own test data".
  • Suggesting the problem back. 2023 Section C Q7b saw students propose one of the two options from the original dilemma.
  • Suggesting removal of the feature rather than redesigning it (2024 Section C Q15b).
  • Confusing security controls with risk-management techniques (2023 Section B Q5b).
What full-mark answers did
  • Proposed something the organisation in the case study could actually do next week.
  • Kept the two suggestions structurally different from each other.
  • Named the technique precisely: penetration testing, software audit, parameterised queries, input sanitisation.

Outline

Usually 2–4 marks 6 items · 2020–2022

Set out the main points or features briefly, without full explanation.

Worth noting. Outline has not appeared as a command term in a written-response question since 2022. It is still named in the general comments of the 2023, 2024 and 2025 reports as a key command term, so it remains examinable — but the recent papers have used Describe and State in its place.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per main point listed, with enough detail to show it is understood — 2021 Section C Q13b awarded one mark for each of three backup-scheme considerations.
  • ReportWhere Outline follows Recommend, the outline marks attach to how the recommendation works (2020 Section C Q14).

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Confusing the purpose of a document with its components. 2020 Section C Q4a found many students listing SRS sections instead of outlining its purpose.
  • Confusing backup with archiving (2021 Section C Q13c).
  • Answering with a single step where the strategy needed several (2022 Section C Q8).
What full-mark answers did
  • Gave a short numbered or bulleted set of distinct points.
  • Covered the number of points asked for exactly — no more, no fewer.

Discuss

Usually 2–3 marks 3 items · 2020–2025

Examine an issue by considering its implications, and reach a reasoned position on it.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for identifying the issue correctly.
  • Report1 mark for its consequence or impact on a named party.
  • Report1 further mark for linking the discussion to the specific material the question supplies — legislation, a project plan, a case-study organisation.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Naming a law without saying what it requires. 2021 Section B Q3 found many students asserting a breach of both Acts without explaining the implication of either.
  • Vague consequences such as going to jail or getting in trouble with police (2021 Section B Q3).
  • Answering an ethics question with legislation. 2024 Section C Q15a explicitly refused marks for citing the Privacy Act 1988 where the question was about ethics.
  • Staying generic. 2024 Section B Q3b wanted the critical path and progress recording tied to the specific delay described.
What full-mark answers did
  • Separated "here is the issue" from "here is who it affects and how".
  • Explained what each piece of legislation actually requires before claiming a breach.
  • Named the affected party from the case study, including reputational impact on the organisation.

Compare

2 marks in both occurrences 2 items · 2020–2025

Set two things side by side and identify how they differ, using the same basis for each.

Rare as a stated term, constant as a requirement. Compare itself appears only twice in six years. But every assessment report from 2020 to 2024 lists discussing all options when asked to justify a choice or compare one option with another among the things students should do — so comparison is examined mostly inside Justify, Explain and Describe items.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for a correct account of the first item, 1 mark for the second, with the difference made explicit.
  • Report2023 Section B Q6 was explicit: students needed a clear definition of both version control and backing up before a comparison was possible.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Defining only one of the two things (2023 Section B Q6).
  • Comparing on different bases — describing what one does and what the other looks like.
  • Not referring to the visible feature that carries the difference. 2024 Section B Q5b wanted the magnifying-glass icon named specifically.
What full-mark answers did
  • Used one sentence per item, on the same criterion, joined by whereas.
  • Named the concrete feature responsible for the difference.

Historical evidence

Classify / Categorise

Usually 2–4 marks 5 items · 2020–2025

Assign each given item to the correct category from a supplied list.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per correctly classified item, or a small number of marks for a fully correct table.
  • PaperAlways a table with a fixed set of categories given in the question or the key.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Using a plausible-sounding category that is not the right one. 2021 Section B Q2 saw usability offered instead of technical, and social instead of legal.
  • Mixing up the taxonomies — 2024 Section C Q2 saw constraints classified as functional or non-functional requirements.
  • Confusing goals with objectives, and organisational with information-system level (2024 Section B Q4).
  • Treating usability as a measure of efficiency. The 2022 report states it is a measure of effectiveness.
What full-mark answers did
  • Worked from the study design's own category definitions rather than everyday meaning.
  • Filled every cell — blanks score zero and there is no penalty for a considered guess.

Select / Circle / Choose

Usually 1 mark for the choice ~14 items · 2020–2025

Pick the correct option or options from those provided.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for the correct selection. A wrong selection usually voids the follow-on marks entirely — 2024 Section B Q5b and 2023 Section C Q9 both refused all marks where the wrong option was chosen.
  • PaperNearly always followed by a second verb: justify, explain, state why. The selection is worth least and matters most.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Backing the wrong option. 2024 Section B Q5b awarded nothing for magnification tool 2, and 2024 Section C Q8b nothing for Design B.
  • Choosing correctly but then discussing something not proposed earlier — 2022 Section C Q16b penalised students who explained a component they had not listed in part a.
  • Leaving the circle or tick ambiguous.
What full-mark answers did
  • Wrote the choice clearly on the supplied line before the reasoning.
  • Carried the same option through every subsequent part of the question.

Recommend

2–4 marks, always paired 4 items · 2020–2025

Advise a specific course of action as the best one available.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark for the recommendation itself, named precisely.
  • Report1 or more marks for the accompanying verb — explain how, outline how — which is where most of the value sits.
  • PaperRecommend has never appeared alone in these six papers. It always carries a second instruction.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Recommending backup or archiving where version control was required (2021 Section C Q10).
  • Recommending a control but not saying how it solves the stated problem (2020 Section C Q14).
  • Recommending improvements only to the symptoms in the stimulus rather than to the process itself (2025 Section C Q13).
What full-mark answers did
  • Named one control and then traced its effect through to the team or user affected.
  • Made the second recommendation genuinely different from the first.

Propose

Usually 2–3 marks 4 items · 2020–2025

Put forward a plan, strategy or approach for a stated purpose.

Typical mark breakdown

  • Report1 mark per named component where the question specifies components — 2023 Section B Q5b gave one mark each for technique, responsibility and timing.
  • Report1 mark per distinct proposal otherwise, with a second mark for how it would work.

Examiner evidence

Where marks were lost
  • Describing the technique again under the "responsibility" heading instead of naming who is responsible (2023 Section B Q5b).
  • Proposing backup or archiving as a security strategy — explicitly not awarded in 2022 Section C Q16a.
  • Proposing something without the mechanism that makes it work.
What full-mark answers did
  • Filled every labelled component line the question provided.
  • Placed the timing inside a named stage of the problem-solving methodology.

List

2 marks in all occurrences 3 items · 2020–2022

Typical mark breakdown

Occasional command terms

Each of these has appeared once or twice since 2020. They are low-frequency but high-certainty: when they appear, the mark is all-or-nothing.

Name · Define · Construct

1–2 marks 3 items · 2020–2025
  • PaperName — 2023 Section C Q1a, 1 mark, the specific development model. ReportThe answer was "spiral"; fewer than half the cohort had it. Name means the exact term and nothing more.
  • PaperDefine — 2022 Section C Q5a, 1 mark, the term "affordance". ReportOnly 22% earned the mark; many defined it in terms of cost and budget. A Define question wants the study-design meaning in one sentence.
  • PaperConstruct — 2023 Section C Q10a, 2 marks, a set of tests for file-extension checking. ReportMarks needed a valid case and an invalid case, each with a realistic file name and its expected result. Answers giving only an extension (.mp4) rather than a file name (Message.mp4) were not awarded.
TeachingConstruct is the sibling of Complete. Complete gives you a partly built artefact; Construct gives you none. Both are marked on coverage — does the set of tests exercise every branch?

Determine · Calculate

1–2 marks 3 items · 2020–2021
  • PaperDetermine — 2021 Section C Q2b (2 marks): decide whether a team should be concerned about falling behind. ReportThe marks went to the reasoning — the dependent task was five weeks away — not to the yes/no.
  • PaperDetermine — 2020 Section C Q10c (1 mark): the number of binary-search iterations needed to establish a value is absent. ReportThe answer was four; the report reminds students to practise searching and sorting algorithms by hand.
  • PaperCalculate — 2021 Section C Q2c (1 mark): the critical path, in weeks. ReportThe answer was 13 weeks, reached by 32% of students. Calculate wants a number in the units the question names.

Write · Rewrite · Draw

1–4 marks ~8 items · 2020–2025
  • PaperWrite — 2024 Section C Q13 (3 marks), pseudocode for a payment function; 2024 Section C Q8a (1 mark), an additional evaluation criterion; 2025 Section C Q4a (4 marks), the four missing DFD labels.
  • PaperRewrite — 2024 C Q12c, 2021 C Q7c and 2020 C Q9c, all 1 mark: rewrite the faulty line of pseudocode. ReportThe whole line must be written out. In 2024 the fix was changing > to >=; answers expressing the operator informally, or reversing it, earned nothing.
  • PaperDraw — 2024 Section C Q3b (1 mark), an association on a use case diagram. ReportMany students left it blank or attached the use case to the wrong actor.
ReportPseudocode conventions are marked, every year. The 2024 report lists the recurring errors directly: the equals sign used for assignment, a double equals used for equivalence, and a missing End If. The 2020 report adds inconsistent operators and unindented code. These are free marks.

What never appears

Verified absent, 2020–2025

Generic VCE command-term lists include these three. None of them appears in a written-response question in any Software Development paper from 2020 to 2025.

EvaluateNot used in six years. Evaluation is examined through Justify against supplied criteria instead.
AnalyseNot used in six years, despite "analysis" being a stage of the problem-solving methodology.
DistinguishNot used. The work is done by Compare and by two-part Explain items.
TeachingDon't spend revision time rehearsing these three. If you meet one in a practice paper from another source, treat Evaluate as Justify against criteria, and Analyse as Explain with consequences.

What VCAA assessment reports consistently reward

Nine patterns drawn from the 2020–2025 assessment reports. Each one is supported by the report evidence that produced it. Note that the 2025 report carries no general-comments section, so the two findings below that rest on general comments are evidenced from 2020 to 2024.

Read the command term, the marks and the number of lines together

Every report from 2020 to 2024 opens with the same advice in slightly different words, and it is the only piece of advice repeated in all five. The number of ruled lines is a deliberate signal about depth, not a coincidence of layout.

Evidence: the general comments of the 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024 reports each name the command term, the mark allocation and the number of lines provided as the guide to the detail required. The 2020 report calls it the "command word", the 2022 report the "question stem", and 2021, 2023 and 2024 the "command term".

Address all options whenever a choice is involved

This is the second piece of advice repeated in every general-comments section from 2020 to 2024. It governs Justify, Compare, and any question that names two alternatives, even when neither word appears.

Evidence: the instruction to discuss all options when justifying a choice or comparing one option with another appears in the 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024 general comments. The 2024 report then repeats it inside two individual questions (C Q5 and C Q10), reminding students that justification requires an explanation of why the alternative is unsuitable.

Two marks means two different ideas

Restating a point in different words is the most frequently penalised behaviour in the whole evidence base, and it costs a mark every time.

Evidence: the 2024 report records it at B Q2 ("two advantages that were synonymous"), C Q7, C Q15b, C Q18a, C Q18b and C Q19c. The 2025 report records it at C Q13. The 2021 report records it at B Q1, the 2020 report at C Q6, and the 2022 report at C Q8 and C Q11b.

In Section C, the case study is part of the answer

Correct theory with no case-study reference is routinely capped below full marks. The case study is a detachable insert precisely so it can sit beside you while you write.

Evidence: 2025 C Q2 and C Q3 (few students referred to the case study for full marks); 2025 C Q10a (only a few related their response to the case study); 2024 C Q14b (few gained the second mark, which was the case-study link); 2021 C Q1; 2023 C Q5, where the report reminds students that the answers were clearly set out in the insert. Every report lists removing the insert among the things students should do.

Use the study design's vocabulary, exactly

Near-synonyms and abbreviations are marked wrong. The examiners are checking whether you hold the subject's terms, not whether you can paraphrase them.

Evidence: 2025 C Q7c refused "Bool" and "Float" and required camel case consistent with the supplied object description; 2024 C Q11 refused invented integrity characteristics such as "consistency"; 2024 B Q6c refused "accuracy" and "reliability" as characteristics of data integrity; 2022 C Q3a refused "the boundary", "the system" and "the box" for the system boundary; 2022 C Q10a found students unable to name the control structures from the study design.

Cite legislation by full name, year and jurisdiction

A statute reference without its year is incomplete, and Victorian and Commonwealth Acts are not interchangeable.

Evidence: the 2020 report, at C Q12a, reminds students to include the correct year, and notes that the Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014 applies only to the Victorian public sector. The 2021 report writes the references in full as the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Health Records Act 2001 (Vic), and notes that few students explained the implications of each Act. The 2023 report, on a multiple-choice item, states plainly that the Health Records Act is Victorian legislation.

Claims must be supported by the stimulus, not assumed

If the question does not give you the information, you cannot rest an argument on it — however reasonable the assumption seems.

Evidence: 2024 C Q18a and C Q18b both refused cost comparisons between eye-tracking and observation because the question supplied no cost information. 2024 C Q15a refused legislative breach claims because it was not clear from the case study that any law had been broken. 2023 C Q13 refused suggestions the scenario made impossible.

Test tables are marked on coverage, and on boundaries

A test table is not a list of plausible inputs. It is a demonstration that every branch and every boundary has been exercised, written in the algorithm's own output strings.

Evidence: the 2020 report, at C Q9a, sets out the rule directly — test above, on and below a numeric boundary, and for a Boolean test both TRUE and FALSE. The 2022 report at C Q12 required every branch of a nested conditional. The 2024 report at C Q12a notes that retesting a value below the boundary was unnecessary because the sample row already covered it. The 2025 report at C Q11c refused repeated test scenarios and abbreviated outputs.

Answer every part, and answer the part that was asked

Compound questions lose marks on the second verb, and blanks lose marks outright. Re-reading the question against your own answer is listed in every report as an expected exam behaviour.

Evidence: 2024 C Q3b records students leaving the association blank; 2024 C Q4b found few students supplying the reason that carried the second mark; 2024 C Q12b penalised line numbers where the line of pseudocode was required; 2024 C Q18b refused disadvantages of the alternative where advantages of the named method were asked for; 2021 C Q8 notes many students did not attempt the question at all.

Student examination checklist

Two passes: one before you write anything, one before you move on. Both take under a minute per question.

Before writing

  • Underline the command term. If there are two verbs, underline both.
  • Note the marks, and count the ruled lines. Plan roughly one distinct point per mark.
  • Decide what the term actually wants: a name, a mechanism, a comparison, a decision, or a completed artefact.
  • Check for a number word — two risks, three reasons — and plan that many separate ideas.
  • If this is Section C, find the detail in the insert you will refer to.
  • If options are named, decide which one you are backing before you start the sentence.
  • If criteria are supplied, list them in the margin so each gets used.

Before moving on

  • Have I answered every labelled part — a., b., c., and every line in the table?
  • Are my two points actually different, or is the second one the first reworded?
  • Have I said why the alternative is less suitable, where a choice was involved?
  • Have I named people, data or figures from the case study?
  • Have I used study-design terms, spelled out in full, rather than near-synonyms?
  • If I cited a law: full name, year, and (Cth) or (Vic)?
  • For pseudocode: assignment arrows, matching End If, consistent indentation?
  • Does my answer fit the lines provided? If it badly overruns, I have probably answered a different question.
