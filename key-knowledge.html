High-Frequency Key Knowledge

Which key knowledge the end-of-year examination actually keeps coming back to, mapped against the study design from 2025.

6 exams analysed 600 marks mapped, all sections 12 priority areas

How this was worked out, and the one big caveat

Every question in the 2020–2025 papers was mapped, mark by mark, onto a key knowledge dot point in the current study design — all three sections, 600 marks in total.

Read this before you use the rankings. Only the 2025 examination was set on the VCE Applied Computing Study Design (from 2025). The 2020–2024 papers were set on the previous study design, and some of what they examined is no longer key knowledge. Those marks have been excluded from the rankings. Frequency tells you where the examiners have historically spent their marks; it does not override the study design. Where the two disagree, the study design wins.

Mark counts are approximate. Compound questions were split across dot points where a question genuinely assessed two things — a test table question that also required an error to be identified, for example, contributes to both testing and debugging.

Section A is included, which matters: multiple-choice is where discrete programming knowledge lives, and adding it moves programming features and algorithms from an afterthought to fourth place. Section A stems were read from the papers directly; 113 of the 120 items are classified, the remainder being items on pages that could not be read from the published PDFs.

On the areas of study. The weighting of each area of study in the examination follows the marks allocated to the outcome it belongs to — 20% Unit 3 Area of Study 1, 30% Unit 3 Area of Study 2, 30% Unit 4 Area of Study 1, 20% Unit 4 Area of Study 2. The ranking below orders priorities within that weighting; it is not a reason to skip an area of study.

Difficulty spread

The analysis shows a consistent spread of question difficulty across papers.

Difficulty bandShare of paperWhat it looks like
Easy25%Recall and classification — State, Identify, Name, Classify, simple Complete. Answerable straight from the study design.
Achievable50%Applied knowledge — Describe, Explain, Suggest, Outline, test tables. Needs the concept plus the scenario. This is the accessible core of the paper, and the largest single band.
Hard25%Extended reasoning — Justify, Discuss, Compare, multi-part design and evaluation items. Needs a position, an alternative, and evidence.

Half of any paper is designed to be accessible — the achievable band, where a student who knows the content and can apply it to the scenario should be scoring. With the easy quarter on top of it, three-quarters of the marks sit inside reach before the hard band is reached at all.

The assessment reports show those marks routinely going begging, and the easy band is where it is most visible: only 17% of the 2025 cohort could state a threat modelling principle, and only 22% could define affordance in 2022. Those are study-design sentences. Securing the easy quarter and then working through the achievable half is a better use of a mid-range student's remaining time than anything in the hard band.

What Section A changes. Multiple choice is where discrete programming knowledge is tested — 41 of the 113 classified Section A items sit in Unit 3 Area of Study 1. Counting it moves programming features, algorithms and OOP from an afterthought in a written-response-only count to fourth overall. Because most of those marks are multiple-choice, the written-response questions in area 4 below are a supplement to past Section A papers, not a replacement for them.
